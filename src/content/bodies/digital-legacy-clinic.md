## What the clinic is

The Digital Legacy Clinic is a student-run help desk at CU Boulder. We do two things. We help people think through their own end-of-life data — the accounts, files, photos, and logins they'll leave behind — and we offer post-mortem support to people managing the digital remains of someone who's died.

Most of what we handle isn't financial. It's the recipe folder, the shared photo library, the email account nobody can get into, the Facebook profile that keeps suggesting birthdays. The work that estate planning forgets to ask about.

## How a session works

People reach us through a ticket on the clinic's website. A small team of student volunteers triages the request, pairs the visitor with whoever's best suited to help, and sits with them for as long as it takes. Sometimes that's an hour. Sometimes it's three sessions over a month. The clinic operates out of the Identity Lab and is co-led with Dr. Jed Brubaker.

## My arc through it

I've been here since the clinic opened in August 2024, and I've worked it from four different angles.

### Fall 2024 — IT

The first semester was infrastructure. We were figuring out what the clinic even was, and somebody had to make the website work, get the intake form wired up, and make sure tickets actually reached the right person. That was me. I learned more about institutional plumbing in those four months than I had in the rest of my degree.

### Spring 2025 — Triage

Once intake was stable, I moved to the front of the queue. Triage is the part of the work where you read a stranger's first message about a parent who just died, or a partner whose iCloud is locked, and decide what kind of help they need before they've fully decided themselves. It's harder than it sounds. It's also where I started noticing patterns I'd later want to write about.

### Fall 2025 — Researcher

I shifted onto the research side and started treating clinic encounters as a site of study, with the same care for consent and framing that any human-subjects work requires. A lot of what's in the *Training for Empathy* paper started here.

### Spring 2026 — Mentor and guest lecturer

This semester I've been mentoring newer volunteers and giving guest lectures in adjacent courses on what the clinic does and what we've learned from it. The point of the rotation, in retrospect, was to make sure I'd done every job in the building before trying to teach anyone else how to do them.

## The research thread

Alongside the service work, we're curating a public-facing research site that pairs an overview of the clinic with the intake form itself, so people arriving in grief don't have to read a literature review to ask for help. The first paper coming out of this work is *Training for Empathy*, currently under review at ACM GROUP 2027, which looks at what student volunteers actually learn from doing this kind of support — and what the clinic, as a teaching environment, is quietly training them to feel.

## What the work keeps showing

The clinic gets framed, when people first hear about it, as a technical service. It isn't really. The technical parts — the locked account, the missing password, the platform that won't recognize a death certificate — are usually the easy parts. The hard part is sitting with someone while they decide what they want to keep.
